# Curate datasets with rights, consent, privacy, and safety controls

A technically valid dataset can still be inappropriate to collect, train on, retain, or distribute. Review authority and risk before copying media into ai-toolkit, and repeat that review before publishing a dataset version, sharing a LoRA, or changing the intended use.

This chapter provides an operational checklist, not legal advice. Copyright, publicity, privacy, biometric, employment, health, child-protection, and data-protection obligations vary by jurisdiction and use. When authority is uncertain or the consequences are meaningful, stop and obtain advice from a qualified person rather than treating a configuration setting as permission.

## Establish rights and license compatibility

For every source, record why you are authorized to obtain it and use it for the intended training. “Available online,” “found by search,” and “generated by a model” are not complete rights records.

Check the actual license or agreement for:

- copying and local storage;
- machine-learning or model-training use;
- modification and creation of derivative material;
- commercial use, if the LoRA or its outputs may be monetized;
- redistribution of source files, frozen dataset snapshots, captions, masks, or controls;
- attribution, notice, share-alike, noncommercial, or field-of-use conditions;
- sublicensing or distribution of trained weights and generated outputs;
- expiration, revocation, geographic, account, or access restrictions.

Keep a copy or durable reference to the license text and its version at acquisition time. A page can change or disappear. Record author or owner, source location, acquisition date, license identifier, required attribution, intended use, and reviewer. Do not combine sources whose terms conflict with the planned dataset, model license, distribution method, or commercial use.

Owning one copy of an image, video, recording, game, film, book, or product does not automatically grant all reproduction and training rights. Likewise, commissioning a photograph or recording does not automatically resolve the rights of the creator, depicted person, performer, venue, music, trademark, or embedded work. Resolve each relevant layer.

Generated media also needs provenance. Record the generating system, its applicable terms, prompts or inputs when safe to retain, and any copyrighted or personal source material used for conditioning. Do not label uncertain output as rights-cleared merely because no human photographer pressed a shutter.

The [prerequisites chapter](../getting-started/prerequisites.md) treats dataset authority as a condition for starting, while [curation](curation.md) explains how to version the approved selection.

## Obtain informed consent

Permission to capture media is not automatically informed consent to train a reusable identity, voice, style, or behavior adapter. Explain the actual project in language the person can understand before collecting or repurposing their data.

A useful consent record states:

- what images, video, audio, text, captions, and derived controls will be used;
- whether the model represents identity, appearance, voice, style, motion, or another attribute;
- who will train, access, evaluate, and receive the dataset or LoRA;
- whether use is private, research, public, or commercial;
- where data and backups are stored and for how long;
- foreseeable misuse, impersonation, editing, or recombination risks;
- whether outputs or weights can be redistributed;
- how to ask questions, restrict use, or request removal;
- which uses require renewed permission.

Consent should be specific, freely given, documented, and obtained from someone able to grant it. Do not rely on consent produced by pressure, a power imbalance, hidden terms, or a purpose materially different from the training now proposed. A person agreeing to a private avatar does not necessarily agree to public weights, sexualized edits, political messaging, advertising, or voice impersonation.

Treat a new audience, modality, concept, or commercial purpose as a scope review. If a planned use exceeds the recorded consent, obtain new permission or exclude the material. Keep contact and consent evidence separate from the training folder with appropriately restricted access.

## Protect privacy and personal data

Minimize collection. Keep only media and metadata needed for the documented learning goal. Remove information that provides no training value but increases harm if exposed.

Inspect more than the visible subject:

- filenames, folder names, captions, manifests, and notes may contain legal names or account identifiers;
- EXIF and media metadata may contain GPS coordinates, timestamps, device identifiers, or creator information;
- backgrounds may reveal addresses, mail, badges, screens, school or workplace details, vehicle plates, medical information, or bystanders;
- audio may reveal names, conversations, location, health, beliefs, relationships, or other people who did not consent;
- cloud paths, logs, examples, screenshots, and YAML may expose private storage structure or credentials.

Strip unnecessary metadata, redact unrelated identifiers, crop or exclude private backgrounds, and avoid personal data in captions and trigger words. Never place access tokens, passwords, private URLs, or secrets in a dataset, caption, job configuration, sample prompt, book example, or smoke record.

Faces, voices, gait, and other distinctive traits may be biometric or otherwise sensitive personal data depending on the jurisdiction and processing. Pseudonymous filenames do not make a recognizable face or voice anonymous. Limit access to raw media, frozen presets, caches, checkpoints, samples, logs, and backups; encryption and access logging may be appropriate for the risk.

Define retention before training. Know which live folders, immutable preset versions, `_latent_cache`, `_t_e_cache`, outputs, backups, and experiment copies contain source-derived information. A deletion plan that covers only the original JPEG but not its copied snapshot, caption, control, cache, or sample is incomplete.

## Handle sensitive content and minors

Use a higher review threshold for intimate or sexual material, medical or disability information, trauma, violence, political or religious activity, precise location, financial information, employment records, and other sensitive content. Exclude it unless the project has a necessary, lawful, consented purpose and safeguards proportionate to the harm.

Do not infer sensitive traits from appearance or caption guesses. A captioning model can produce confident but false labels about identity, health, ethnicity, gender, age, relationships, criminality, or beliefs. Remove such unsupported labels and review any annotation that could stigmatize a person.

Minors require special protection. Do not build an identity, face, voice, or intimate-content dataset involving a minor without verified authority, informed involvement appropriate to age, a clearly legitimate purpose, strict access and distribution limits, and any professional review required by the applicable rules. Never include sexual or exploitative content involving minors. When age is uncertain in a sensitive context, exclude the source and escalate the review.

Also review bystanders, crowds, reflections, background voices, and control inputs. Consent from the primary subject does not automatically cover every identifiable person embedded in the file. Blurring a face may be insufficient when clothing, voice, location, or context remains identifying.

Plan misuse evaluation for identity and voice LoRAs. Test impersonation risk, misleading contexts, nonconsensual sexualization, harassment, fraud, and bypass of expected activation controls. Do not publish weights merely because the intended samples look harmless.

## Preserve provenance and honor removal

Attach a rights and safety record to each dataset version. The manifest should make it possible to trace every selected source without putting unnecessary personal data into the training job itself.

Record:

- stable item ID and content digest;
- source and acquisition date;
- owner, creator, subject, or consent record references where applicable;
- license and restrictions;
- review status, reviewer, and decision date;
- transformations, captions, masks, controls, and generated derivatives;
- approved purposes, audience, retention, and distribution limits;
- removal or expiration status.

Immutable ai-toolkit dataset presets help reproduce what a job consumed, but immutability is not a reason to ignore a valid removal obligation. Mark the affected version unavailable according to the project policy, publish a new version without the item, and locate every managed copy. Preserve only the minimum audit evidence that may lawfully remain.

A removal workflow should consider live source, source-missing retained files, preset snapshots, manifests, captions, masks, controls, latent and text-embedding caches, backups, samples, shared archives, and downstream recipients. Document what was removed, when, by whom, and what could not be recalled.

Deleting source data does not reliably remove its influence from an already trained LoRA. If continued use is no longer authorized, stop distribution and use of affected weights; assess whether retraining from an approved dataset version is required. Do not promise technical “unlearning” unless it has actually been performed and validated.

The caption audit in [captions and triggers](captions-and-triggers.md) should use stable item IDs so a corrected label or removal can be traced through dataset versions without exposing personal names in training text.

## Run a preflight review

Before queueing a job, require an explicit answer to every item:

- The intended concept, users, outputs, and distribution are documented.
- Every source has a recorded authority and license compatible with that use.
- Consent covers training, identity or voice use, audience, and redistribution where relevant.
- Minors, bystanders, and sensitive content received the required heightened review or were excluded.
- Captions and triggers contain no unnecessary personal data, secrets, or unsupported sensitive labels.
- Metadata and backgrounds were inspected and minimized.
- The dataset version and manifest identify every selected derivative and its provenance.
- Storage, access, retention, incident response, and removal procedures are assigned to people, not left implicit.
- Model and base-weight licenses are compatible with the intended LoRA distribution.
- A misuse evaluation and release decision are planned before weights leave the controlled environment.

If any answer is unknown, pause the job. Create a quarantined review area rather than leaving questionable media mixed into the training folder. Record the eventual include/exclude decision so the same uncertainty does not recur in the next version.

Re-run preflight when data, consent, license terms, jurisdiction, model, product purpose, audience, or distribution changes. A passed review applies to a defined version and use; it is not permanent blanket permission.
